Problem setup

Two observations, one hidden source

The observed systems contain their own dynamics plus a shared imprint from an unobserved driver.

  • Observed time series: x and y.
  • Hidden process: z, a common driver.
  • Time-delay embedding turns scalar histories into reconstructed state spaces.
  • The reconstruction target is the latent trajectory, up to an admissible transformation.

Mapper-Coach intuition

Prediction pressure turns a bottleneck into a latent estimate

MaCo learns to predict one driven system using its own past and information extracted from the other driven system.

  • The mapper compresses the reconstructed state of one observation.
  • The coach uses that bottleneck to predict the other observation.
  • When prediction succeeds, the bottleneck aligns with the hidden driver.
In the cited MaCo paper, this was demonstrated on coupled chaotic logistic maps. 04
ASOM / AniSOM intuition

Manifold learning can separate shared from private dynamics

The ASOM paper frames the same hidden-driver question through time-delay embedding, intrinsic dimension, and anisotropic SOM training.

  • The observed attractor is treated as a structured manifold.
  • Anisotropic training emphasizes directions associated with common dynamics.
  • The resulting map provides an unsupervised, geometry-first baseline.

Experiment design

Three families probe different dynamical regimes

  • Logistic maps: discrete chaotic maps with known hidden common input.
  • Tent maps: another nonlinear discrete-map family with sharper behavior.
  • Lorenz systems: continuous-flow setting with harder reconstruction geometry.

Main result

MaCo leads on maps; DCCA leads on Lorenz

The result pattern matches the story: prediction bottlenecks shine in map settings; continuous flows are more contested. 07

The headline is not one method wins everywhere. It is that hidden-driver reconstruction depends strongly on the geometry of the observed system.

  • MaCo has the clearest advantage on discrete chaotic maps.
  • ASOM is consistently the strongest manifold baseline on map families.
  • Lorenz elevates DCCA and narrows the MaCo/CCA gap.

Robustness

MaCo is stable with moderate data and low-to-mid noise

The robustness figure answers a practical question: when does reconstruction remain useful?

  • Performance is already strong at length 100 and stabilizes around length 300 and beyond.
  • Low-noise reconstructions stay near the high-performance regime.
  • Increasing noise produces a smooth degradation rather than an abrupt failure.

Interpretation

The experiments connect two complementary ideas

Prediction can reveal latent structure

The MaCo results support the Mapper-Coach claim: a minimal predictive bottleneck can align with the common hidden driver.

Geometry remains competitive

ASOM/AniSOM offers a strong unsupervised manifold baseline, especially on discrete chaotic maps.

Continuous flows are harder

Lorenz results show that method ranking changes when the dynamics and embeddings become more complex.

Together, the papers motivate both neural and geometric approaches to hidden-driver inference. 11
Takeaways and references

Three messages to leave with

  • Hidden common drivers are recoverable when their imprint is redundantly encoded in observed dynamics.
  • MaCo is the strongest method on the discrete-map benchmarks in this repo.
  • Lorenz and noise analyses show where reconstruction becomes more demanding.
